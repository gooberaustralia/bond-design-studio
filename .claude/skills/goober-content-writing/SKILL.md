# Website Copywriting

This skill produces website copy that does three jobs at once: converts human visitors, ranks in Google, and gets cited by AI search engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews). Every page written with this skill must serve all three. If copy only does one, it fails.

The standard is copy that reads like it was written by someone who deeply understands the business, not by a template. Generic copy is the enemy. "Our Services" headings, "We pride ourselves on quality", "Contact us today" with no context. None of that survives this skill.

## Phase 1: Understand Before Writing (Mandatory)

Never write a word of copy until you can answer all of these. If the information is not in the conversation, project files, or the client's existing website, ASK. Do not invent facts about a real business.

**The Business**
- What exactly do they do, and what do they NOT do?
- What is their business type and industry? (A plumber, a law firm, and a skincare clinic need completely different copy registers)
- Who is the ideal customer? Be specific: a 38 year old mum on the Gold Coast searching "emergency dentist near me" at 9pm is a different reader to a procurement manager comparing B2B suppliers.
- What does the customer feel before they arrive? Stressed? Curious? Sceptical? Price shopping? In pain? The emotional starting point determines the opening lines.
- What is the customer actually buying? Nobody buys "air conditioning installation". They buy a cool house in February. Nobody buys "conveyancing". They buy not getting screwed on the biggest purchase of their life.

**The Brand**
- What is the brand's personality? Pick 3 to 4 traits (e.g. warm and straight talking, premium and quietly confident, playful and local). Every sentence on the site should sound like the same person wrote it.
- What is the brand's point of view? What do they believe that competitors don't say? A strong opinion is the cheapest differentiation that exists.
- Are there words and phrases the brand uses? Are there words it would never use?

**The Goal**
- What is the ONE primary action for each page? Call, book, quote form, purchase, enquiry. One page, one job.
- What objections stand between the reader and that action? Price, trust, time, "will this work for me", "are they local", "have they done this before". The copy must answer these before the reader has to ask.

**The Competitive Landscape**
- What do the top ranking competitors say? The goal is never to copy them. It is to find what they all say so we can say something different, and to find what none of them answer so we can answer it (this is the "information gain" that both Google and LLMs reward).

If you have web search available, research the industry, common customer questions ("people also ask" style queries), and competitor positioning before drafting.

## Phase 2: Structure the Page as a Story

A web page is not a brochure. It is a guided walk. The reader scrolls down a single path and every section must earn the next scroll. Structure each page as a narrative arc:

1. **Hero: name the reader's world.** The H1 plus opening lines must do three things in under 3 seconds: tell the reader they are in the right place, signal what the business does (with the primary keyword present naturally), and give a reason to keep going. Lead with the outcome the customer wants, not the company name or "Welcome to".
2. **The problem or desire, in their words.** Show the reader you understand their situation better than they can articulate it. Use the actual language customers use (reviews, enquiry emails, and forums are gold for this).
3. **The shift: what's possible.** Introduce the service as the bridge from where they are to where they want to be.
4. **How it works / what's included.** Concrete, specific, scannable. This is where features live, always tied back to benefits. "Same day quotes" matters because "you're not waiting around wondering."
5. **Proof.** Testimonials, results, numbers, credentials, years, locations served, real photos referenced. Proof placed immediately after claims, not dumped in one section at the bottom.
6. **Objection handling.** Pricing context, guarantees, process transparency, FAQs. The FAQ section pulls double duty: it converts hesitant readers AND it is prime real estate for LLM citations and featured snippets.
7. **The close.** A clear, specific, low friction call to action with a reason to act now that is honest (availability, timelines, seasonal relevance), never fake urgency.

CTAs appear more than once: one in or near the hero, one mid page after the proof, one at the close. Each CTA repeats the value, not just the verb. "Book your free site assessment" beats "Submit". "See pricing and inclusions" beats "Learn more".

### Headings that read like a story

Section headings are the page's skeleton and the most read words after the H1. They must work as a standalone narrative: a visitor skimming only the headings should understand the offer, feel the personality, and know what to do next.

Banned heading patterns: "Our Services", "About Us", "Why Choose Us", "What We Do", "Testimonials", "Get In Touch".

Instead, headings carry meaning and keywords:
- "Our Services" becomes "Air Conditioning Installation, Repairs and Servicing Across the Gold Coast"
- "Why Choose Us" becomes "Why Gold Coast Families Have Trusted Us for 15 Years"
- "Testimonials" becomes "What Happened When They Called Us"
- "Get In Touch" becomes "Tell Us About Your Project. We'll Reply Today."

The craft rule: each heading should contain either a keyword, a benefit, a proof point, or personality. Ideally two of those. Never zero.

Heading hierarchy is strict and technical: exactly one H1 per page containing the primary keyword. H2s for major sections, H3s nested under H2s, never skipped levels. One topic per section. Both Google and LLM retrieval systems parse pages by heading structure, so a clean hierarchy is an SEO requirement, not a style preference.

## Phase 3: Write for Conversion

- **Clarity beats cleverness, always.** If a clever line costs a moment of confusion, kill it. The reader should never have to re-read a sentence.
- **Benefits first, features as evidence.** Lead with how life improves, then prove it with the feature.
- **You > we.** Aim for the reader's "you" to outnumber the brand's "we". Count if unsure.
- **Specificity sells.** "Most jobs done in under 4 hours" beats "fast service". "Over 2,300 Gold Coast homes" beats "many happy customers". Specifics are also more quotable by LLMs.
- **One idea per sentence, one theme per paragraph.** Paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences on the web. Vary sentence length so the rhythm feels human: a longer explanatory sentence, then a short punchy one. Like that.
- **Emotion drives, logic justifies.** Open sections with the emotional truth, close them with the rational proof the reader needs to justify the decision.
- **Address objections inside the copy**, not only in an FAQ. The word "because" is powerful: "Try us risk free, because if we don't show up on time, the callout is free."
- **Reading level:** roughly grade 6 to 8 for consumer audiences, slightly higher for B2B/professional, but never academic. Short words win. "Use" not "utilise". "Help" not "facilitate".

## Voice and Style Rules (Non Negotiable)

- **Australian English** spelling and idiom throughout (organise, colour, enquiry, specialise) unless the client explicitly targets another market.
- **No dashes as punctuation.** No hyphens used as sentence breaks, no en dashes, no em dashes. Restructure the sentence with a comma, colon, full stop, or parentheses instead. Hyphens are only permitted inside genuinely hyphenated compound words (e.g. "long-term") and even then prefer alternatives where natural.
- **No AI tells.** The full vocabulary, phrase, construction and structure lists, the detector's severities and the scoring gate live in `references/voice.md`, read before every editing pass and at every review. The ten most common on their own would already sink a page: "In today's fast-paced world", "Look no further", "Whether you're a X or a Y", "Welcome to [Brand]", "We pride ourselves", "Quality you can trust", "unlock", "seamless", "elevate", "game-changer". Any sentence that could be pasted onto a competitor's site without edits fails regardless of which list it's on.
- **No fake urgency, no invented statistics, no fabricated testimonials, no claims the client hasn't verified.** Trust is the whole game; one false claim poisons E-E-A-T and the brand.
- **Personality with restraint.** Character shows in word choice, rhythm, honesty, and point of view. Not in exclamation marks, forced jokes, or trying too hard. One memorable line per section beats ten quirky ones. If the brand is playful, be playful in 20% of sentences and clear in 100% of them.
- **Read it aloud test.** If a sentence wouldn't be said by a real person at the business, rewrite it.

## Final Self Check Before Delivering

Run every page against this list. Fix anything that fails:

1. Could the H1 and headings alone tell the story and sell the offer?
2. Does every heading carry a keyword, benefit, proof point, or personality?
3. Is there exactly one primary action, with a CTA at top, middle, and end?
4. Does each major section open with a direct, quotable answer?
5. Would the ideal customer feel "this is for me" within 3 seconds?
6. Is there at least one specific number, detail, or opinion competitors don't have?
7. Does it sound like THIS brand, and pass the read aloud test?
8. Primary keyword in H1, first 100 words, one H2, meta title?
9. 2 to 5 internal links with descriptive anchors?
10. Zero dashes as punctuation, zero banned phrases, Australian English?
11. Could any paragraph be pasted onto a competitor site? If yes, rewrite it.
12. FAQ answers self contained enough to be cited by an LLM verbatim?
