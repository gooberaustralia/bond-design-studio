# Copy: reader psychology

Read when writing the copy brief and again before headlines are drafted. About 900 tokens. Owns the reader model this system writes from: the Rule of One, the awareness and sophistication ladder, and registers by business type. Page structure and conversion craft live in `SKILL.md`; editing, the detector and the scoring gate live in `references/voice.md`; ranking and citation rules live in `references/seo-geo.md`.

## The Rule of One

Fill this in `COPY-BRIEF.md` before drafting a word; every later decision checks back against it.

| One | Write it as | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | A single person with a suburb, a device and an emotional state | Can you picture them reading it on their phone? |
| Big idea | The single thought the page exists to plant | Say it in one sentence with no "and" |
| Promise | The strongest outcome, with a number, a speed or a rate where verified | Is it falsifiable? |
| Offer | What they get, for what, on what terms | Could a stranger repeat it back? |
| CTA | One primary action per page | Is a second action competing? Demote it to transitional |
| Transformation | The belief the reader must change before acting | "Before they think X, after they think Y" |
| Voice | The We Are list, two or three attributes leading | Cover the logo: can the reader still tell who wrote it? |

## Awareness and sophistication

Name both per page before the H1 is written.

| Awareness | The reader | The opening |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know they have the problem | Identity or a feeling they recognise |
| Problem-aware | Knows the pain, not the fix | Names the problem in their words, then the shift |
| Solution-aware | Knows fixes exist, not this one | Shows why this mechanism differs |
| Product-aware | Knows this business, isn't convinced | Leads with objections, proof, comparison |
| Most-aware | Ready to act | Leads with offer, price, availability, phone in the first screen |

Sophistication runs from a plain claim ("we fix hot water") through a bigger claim, then needs the mechanism, then the mechanism elaborated with proof, then identification (who the reader is). Most Australian trades, beauty, real estate and legal markets sit at stage 3 to 5, where a bare claim like "24/7 emergency plumbing" has already been said by every competitor and the reader discounts it; the H1 or subhead wants a mechanism ("fixed price quoted before we lift a tool") or an identification instead.

## Registers by business type

Set tone dials in the brief, then refine with the voice table (method in `references/voice.md`).

| Business type | Register |
|---|---|
| Trades | Short and plain, dry humour only in subheads, never the emergency hero |
| Childcare | Medium length, warm, gentle humour about the kids, never the parents |
| Beauty and premium | Short to medium, confident, wit welcome but never at the client's expense |
| Legal and finance | Calm and plain, one dry line at most |
| Health | Short, warm and calm, light humour outside symptom sections |
| Hospitality | Short and sensory |
| Real estate | Confident and direct |
| SaaS | Plain and confident, with personality in microcopy |
| Ecommerce | Short, sensory and practical |
