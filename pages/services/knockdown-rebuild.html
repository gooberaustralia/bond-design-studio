Knockdown rebuild design in Sydney

Stay in the street you love, in a home designed for how you live now rather than how someone lived in 1985.

The short version

Many homeowners love where they live but find their existing home no longer suits their lifestyle. A knockdown rebuild lets you stay in the location you love while creating a home designed for how you live today.

We guide clients through site analysis, concept design, planning approvals, documentation and construction coordination, to create homes that make the most of their land.

Rebuild or renovate

Which one is your house?

Most people arrive undecided. The honest answer usually comes from the existing house rather than from a preference, and it is worth settling before anyone draws anything.

Rebuilding tends to win when Renovating tends to win when
The house The layout fights you everywhere and the bones are ordinary There is character worth keeping, or a sound original structure
The block The house sits badly on the land, wrong orientation, wasted yard The house is well placed and only part of it fails
The scope You would end up touching almost every room anyway One or two zones need work, the rest is fine
Approvals A new home can often use the faster pathway Alterations to an existing home can be simpler to approve

If the answer turns out to be renovate, we will say so. There is a whole page on renovations and extensions.

The part nobody explains

CDC or DA

Your approval pathway affects the timeline more than any other single decision, and it is decided by your block, not by you.

In New South Wales a new house is approved one of two ways. Which one applies is worked out early, because it changes the design, the drawings and how long you wait.

Complying development Development application
Who assesses it Council or a private certifier, against a fixed set of state standards Your council, against its own local planning controls
The design Must meet every standard. Miss one and the pathway closes Can argue merit, so unusual sites and unusual houses are possible
Neighbours Generally not notified Usually notified, and may object
Timeline The faster of the two, by a wide margin Longer, and varies considerably between councils
When you are stuck with a DA Heritage items and conservation areas, bushfire and flood prone land, some environmentally sensitive blocks, and any design that does not meet the standards

We check which pathway your block can use before design starts, because designing a house that just misses the standards is an expensive way to learn this.

Common questions

Knockdown
rebuilds

What does a knockdown rebuild cost in Sydney?
It depends on the size and finish of the new home and on the block itself. Slope, rock, tree protection, asbestos in the old house and the approval pathway all move the number, sometimes by a lot. We would rather look at your site and give you a real range than quote an average that turns out to be wrong for you.
How much does the design cost?
For a complete design service, fees typically commence from 10% plus GST of the construction value, set finally by the scale and complexity of the project. They are invoiced progressively against work completed. The full fee structure is on the process page.
Do I need council approval, or can I use a certifier?
Both exist. If your block and your design meet the state standards you can use complying development, which is assessed by a certifier and is much faster. If not, it goes to council as a development application. We work out which applies before the design starts.
Can I keep the pool, or the big tree?
Often yes, and it is worth raising on day one. Existing pools, mature trees and retaining walls all constrain where the new house can sit, so they belong in the site analysis rather than in a change request halfway through.
What happens with asbestos?
Anything built before the 1990s may contain it. It does not stop a rebuild, but it does need licensed removal and it should sit in your budget from the start rather than arriving as a surprise at demolition.
Do I need a builder before I start designing?
No. Design comes first, then the documentation lets you tender the project and compare builders on the same drawings. If you already have a builder you want to use, we will work with them.
